# Window design hill-climb: tasks, scores and the keep rule

Written before round 1 and not changed after. Spike only (window-explore); nothing here merges.

## The tasks

A PM opens the window for these (0027's Why). Each has one ground truth per world, computed by
`truth()` in `world.ts` from the world's data, never from a design.

| #   | Task (as the judge reads it)                                                                                             | Ground truth                                                                                          | Judged                      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| T1  | How many threads are waiting on you right now, and which one has waited longest?                                         | threads that are working and wait on the user (held, review, reply); the one with the oldest activity | yes                         |
| T2  | Since you last looked, which workstream had the most of its items change, and how many?                                  | per open workstream, distinct members with a change-log entry after last-seen                         | yes                         |
| T3  | Which open workstream looks stalled: nothing moved in it for the longest?                                                | open workstream whose latest member update or thread activity is oldest                               | yes                         |
| T4  | What is the most important thing that isn't in any workstream yet? Name it and its source. Then make a workstream of it. | planted: an epic no workstream holds that is biggest, most active and most referenced                 | yes (name); measured (make) |
| T5  | Find item `<key>`. What is its status, and is it in a workstream?                                                        | the item's state and its workstream                                                                   | measured only               |
| T6  | What is `<person>` working on right now: how many items in progress, and in which workstream are most of them?           | person's in-progress items; the workstream holding most of them                                       | yes                         |
| T7  | Which item is the team's chat talking about most this week?                                                              | item with the most references from chat messages in the last 7 days (planted inside a workstream)     | yes                         |

T5 is measured only: an answer behind a search box can't be read off a screenshot.

## Worlds and widths

- `fixtures`: the repo's fixtures as they are (SYN, the Slack channel, the folder tree), with a few
  workstreams and threads laid on top, as `pnpm shots` does.
- `medium`: about 600 items, 5 sources, 12 workstreams, about 30 threads.
- `large`: about 4,000 items, 10 sources of different shapes, 40 workstreams, about 120 threads in
  every state.

Generated by a seeded PRNG, no network, no clock. Widths: laptop 1280×800, narrow window 600×800.

## Measured scores (playwright-core, installed Chrome, 4× CPU throttle)

At each world × width:

- **ttfup**: ms from navigation to the first `[data-useful]` element painted (today's window: its first
  cluster row).
- **dom**: elements on the page once loaded.
- **screens**: page height ÷ viewport height.
- **visible**: of T1–T4, T6, T7, how many have their answer on the first screen: the truth's strings
  (name, and count where the task asks one) inside one element of at most 400 characters, inside the
  viewport. A proxy: visible isn't the same as identified.
- **steps**: per task, the clicks and typed strings of the design's scripted path to the answer
  (typing a query is 1). A task with no path is "no path". Reported as a sum with the no-path count.

## Judged scores (fresh subagents)

- Per candidate, a judge per task (T1–T4, T6, T7) per view: large × laptop, large × narrow,
  medium × laptop. 18 judges.
- A judge sees only screenshots: the first screen, and the next three screens as the page scrolls.
  Nothing else: not the world, not the code, not my reasoning. Candidates are labelled A, B, C… by
  a shuffle, so judges can't tell the newest.
- A judge answers in a fixed JSON shape and gives its confidence (0–100). Answers are graded by
  script against the truth: 1 for each correct part, halves where a task has two parts.
  **answered** = the sum over the 18, out of 18.
- One fresh design reviewer per round sees the candidate's and the current best's screenshots at all
  views, labelled, and scores each 1–5 against the rubric below, with a sentence each.

### Reviewer rubric

1. Scanning: can the eye find "what needs me" and "what changed" in two seconds?
2. Hierarchy: is the most important thing the most prominent?
3. Density: is the space used for information rather than chrome, without crowding?
4. Consistency: do like things look alike (rows, counts, states, sources)?
5. Growth: what grows badly as sources reach 10 and workstreams 40 (or 400)?

## The keep rule

A candidate replaces the current best only if **both**:

1. its **answered** is strictly higher than the best's; and
2. on the large world, at both widths, it doesn't lose on the measured scores: ttfup no more than
   10% or 100 ms worse (whichever is larger), dom no more than 10% larger, screens no more than
   10% or 0.5 more, visible not fewer, and no task gains a "no path".

The reviewer's scores inform the next round's idea; they never decide a keep.
