Oriel · B · Ink and signal

Colour is a budget spent only on what needs you: the window is ink on paper, states are shapes, and a single vermilion marks the things waiting on the user, so the eye goes there first.

Words someone wrote (the agent's reply, a ticket's description, a message) are set in a serif; everything Oriel draws from its own data (chips, states, blocks, controls) is sans and mono, so you can tell a claim from a fact by its type.

4 · Motion

--duration-quick for a state changing in place (hover, a row's actions, a chevron); --duration-move for something arriving (the panel, the sidebar, the window chat), both on --ease . Under prefers-reduced-motion, --duration-move is 0: the panel fades without sliding, the sidebar and rows jump, and the working dot stops pulsing.

5 · Icons

16 px on a 16 grid, a 1.5 stroke, round ends and joins, no fill; drawn in the text colour beside them. At 32 px the stroke stays 1.5 px on screen.

6 · Density

One rule: Oriel's own data is compact, words are comfortable. Every list of records (the overview, Browse, rails, blocks) keeps a 2 rem row in sans; prose (a reply, a description, a message) gets the serif at --text-lg and --leading-prose, a 40 rem measure, whatever surface it is on.
